1. 
PRODUCT 
VISION Stonky x Cash Cows is a client-specific investment research and decision engine built for Laura Gao. The platform must answer a more sophisticated question than: "Is this a good stock, ETF or bond?" It must answer: "Is this a high-quality investment, and does adding it to Laura Gao's portfolio improve her probability of achieving her specific financial goals?" Every candidate investment receives two independent scores: Security Quality Score, SQS 0 to 100 Measures how attractive the security is independently of Laura. Laura Portfolio Fit Score, LPFS 0 to 100 Measures how appropriate that investment is for Laura's goals, liabilities, timeline and existing portfolio. These are then combined into: Cash Cow Composite Score, CCS 0 to 100 This is the final headline score. However, a high composite score can never override: 1. a Red Gate failure; 2. poor client fit; 3. inadequate data quality; 4. a violation of the team's portfolio constraints. The product is therefore a decision-support engine , not a return predictor. 

2. 
WHY 
THIS 
SYSTEM 
EXISTS The Wharton competition explicitly emphasizes developing and implementing a coherent long-term strategy rather than winning through short-term trading performance. WInS is the implementation environment, while the strategy, research, decisions and articulation are what matter. Wharton Global Youth Program This means Cash Cows should avoid six team members independently finding securities they like and later inventing justifications for them. Instead: Client objectives → portfolio strategy → evaluation framework → security research → score → committee decision → trade → monitoring → review Every security should pass through the same standardized process. The framework must be locked before candidate scores are used for final selection. This prevents "backfitting", where weights are changed simply to make a preferred investment win. Any later change to weights creates a new framework version and automatically triggers rescoring of all previously evaluated candidates.

9. 
THE 
RED GATE The Red Gate occurs before numerical scoring. A security that fails a hard Red Gate cannot receive a GREEN signal regardless of its numerical score. Universal hard failures Reject if: ● the security is outside the competition's permitted universe for Competition Mode; ● essential data are missing or unreliable; ● a proposed trade breaches a team-approved hard concentration limit; ● the proposed security uses undisclosed leverage; ● the product is an inverse or leveraged ETF unless explicitly permitted and justified; ● the investment thesis cannot be supported with verifiable evidence; ● the investment would reduce modeled operating-funding probability below the team's required threshold; ● the investment creates a portfolio liquidity problem incompatible with Laura's future obligations. Liability-matching hard failures A security intended to fund a mandatory payment fails if: ● its expected cash flow does not adequately match the liability date; ● material default risk is inconsistent with the team's required certainty; ● the security can mature after the required payment date without another defined liquidity source; ● material call risk can remove the expected cash flow before it is needed; ● it is denominated in an inappropriate currency without an acceptable hedge; ● it is a perpetual bond fund being treated as if it has a fixed maturity. Important rule A normal bond ETF does not automatically guarantee a future $50,000 payment because its NAV at a particular future date is unknown. Defined-maturity bond ETFs and individual bonds require separate treatment. FINRA identifies interest-rate risk, duration risk, call risk, reinvestment risk and credit risk as material considerations in bond analysis. It also distinguishes YTM from coupon rate and recommends considering yield to worst for callable securities. FINRA

12. 
NORMALIZATION 
ENGINE Raw financial numbers cannot simply be added together. A 25% operating margin may be exceptional in one industry and ordinary in another. Therefore, quantitative security metrics should usually be measured relative to an appropriate peer group . For stocks: Industry + market-cap bucket + geographic market. For ETFs: ETF category and strategy peer group. For bonds: Credit-quality bucket + maturity bucket + currency. Percentile scoring For metrics where higher is better: Metric Score = percentile rank × 100 For metrics where lower is better: Metric Score = (1 - percentile rank) × 100 Values should be winsorized around the 5th and 95th percentiles to reduce distortion from extreme outliers. Score interpretation Score Interpretation 90 to 100 Exceptional 80 to 89 Strong 70 to 79 Above average 50 to 69 Neutral / acceptable 30 to 49 Weak 0 to 29 Serious weakness If peer data are unavailable, use documented threshold bands.

MONTE

40. 
CASH 
COW 
RISK STATE Laura's risk capacity is not static. Define three modes. 
GROWTH MODE Trigger: ● at least 4 years remain until 2033; ● AND funding probability ≥97%. Behavior: ● growth exposure receives less downside penalty; ● incremental growth is valuable; ● diversification remains important. 
BALANCED MODE Trigger: ● 2 to 4 years remain; ● OR funding probability is 95% to 97%. Behavior: ● liability protection receives greater weight; ● marginal high-risk investments become harder to justify. 
PROTECTION MODE Trigger: ● fewer than 2 years remain; ● OR funding probability <95%; ● OR the 2031 sponsor commitment cannot be credibly supported. Behavior: ● liabilities, liquidity and downside protection dominate; ● speculative growth investments should rarely receive GREEN. This is much stronger than simply saying: "We'll get safer around 2033." The framework mathematically changes behavior. 

41. 
CASH 
COW 
COMPOSITE SCORE The final score should not use a simple average. Client fit should matter slightly more than standalone quality, and an extremely weak score in either dimension should be difficult to hide. Use a weighted geometric mean: CCS = 100 × (SQS / 100)^0.45 × (LPFS / 100)^0.55 Therefore: ● SQS weight = 45%; ● LPFS weight = 55%. This deliberately makes Laura fit more important than generic attractiveness . The geometric structure also punishes imbalance. Example: SQS = 98 LPFS = 65 A simple average could misleadingly make the investment look attractive. The Cash Cow system should recognize: fantastic security, wrong client.

62. ANTI-
BACKFIT RULE The framework weights are version controlled. When weights change: 1. create new version; 2. record author; 3. record date; 4. record reason; 5. automatically rescore all candidates. Never silently adjust weights after seeing candidate scores. This is one of the strongest competition features because it demonstrates a genuine investment process.
